# Thailand Supreme Court rules bitcoin is property, 28 Oct 2026

Fact check of the viral claim "Thailand's Supreme Court officially recognizes Bitcoin as legal property": broadly true, with a caveat. The ruling treats bitcoin as property under Thai criminal law in a robbery case. It does not make bitcoin money or legal tender.

Facts (checked 30 Oct 2026):
- The Crypto Times, 28 Oct 2026: "Thailand's Supreme Court, in Judgment No. 4929/2569, ruled that cryptocurrency is property that can be the object of a robbery offense under Thai criminal law".
- Thaiger (Thai), 28 Oct 2026: headline "ศาลฎีกาชี้ บิตคอยน์เป็นทรัพย์ เพราะมีมูลค่า แลกเงินได้" (Supreme Court: bitcoin is property because it has value and can be exchanged for money).
- ForkLog, 29 Oct 2026: attack on 15 Sep 2022 on Samui; the victim exchanged $50,000 (about 1.878 million baht) for 2.480801 BTC sent to the robbers; the defence argued bitcoin is "electronic data without intrinsic value"; the judges noted market value, ownership and exchangeability for cash; the court considered a Bank of Thailand letter saying digital assets are not legal tender; the 10-year sentence for a man who helped the group was upheld and the BTC ordered returned.
- CoinDesk (29 Oct) reports a plenary bench; not screenshotted (bot check on CoinDesk).

## instagram

Thailand's Supreme Court has ruled that bitcoin counts as property under Thai criminal law (Judgment No. 4929/2569, 28 October, reported by The Crypto Times and Thaiger).

The case: on Samui island in September 2022, a man was threatened and forced to exchange $50,000 for 2.480801 BTC and send it to an address controlled by the robbers (ForkLog). A man who helped the group was sentenced to 10 years and ordered to return the bitcoin.

His appeal argued bitcoin is "electronic data without intrinsic value." The judges rejected that, noting bitcoin has market value, can be owned and can be exchanged for cash (ForkLog).

The court also considered a Bank of Thailand letter saying digital assets are not legal tender. The ruling treats bitcoin as property in this case; it does not make it money.

Sources: The Crypto Times, Thaiger, ForkLog. Photo: Chainwit, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons.
