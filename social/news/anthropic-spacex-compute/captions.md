# Anthropic will pay SpaceX about $3.1 billion a month for compute (S-1, 26 Oct 2026)

Facts (verified):
- Anthropic S-1, filed 26 Oct 2026 (sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/anthropicpbc-sx1.htm), contractual obligations section: "Between May and October 2026, we entered into and amended several agreements with SpaceX.AI LLC (formerly known as X.AI LLC) ("SpaceXAI"), a wholly owned subsidiary of Space Exploration Technologies Corp. ... to access compute powered by NVIDIA GPUs. Under the SpaceXAI Arrangements, we agreed to spend an aggregate of up to $120.5 billion over terms extending into 2029, consisting of approximately $3.1 billion in the aggregate per month once all capacity has been delivered, which we expect in December 2026, with different fees during capacity ramping periods. Following initial non-cancelable periods ending in January 2027 and March 2027 under the respective agreements, each agreement may be cancelled by either party upon 90 days' advance written notice."
- Same section, other compute commitments: Google at least approximately $114.5B (Apr 2026 to Jul 2033), Amazon at least $110.0B (May 2026 to Apr 2036), Microsoft at least $44.9B (Nov 2026 to May 2033), all owed regardless of actual usage; AMD expected to exceed $20.0B if the full initial capacity is procured (no fixed commitment).
- Anthropic S-1: revenue $16.3B in the six months to 30 Jun 2026. OnFin calculation: 6 x $3.1B = $18.6B; 12 x $3.1B = about $37B a year.
- FT via The Star (15 Oct 2026): Anthropic Q3 2026 revenue $16.8B (reported, not in the S-1; the S-1 leaves the Q3 figure blank).
- SpaceX 10-Q for the quarter to 30 Sep 2026, filed 22 Oct 2026, Note 3 (concentration of risk): Customer B was 34.5% of consolidated revenue in Q3 2026 (not above 10% in Q3 2025); "revenue from Customer B relates to the SI segment". Q3 revenue $14,575M, so Customer B is about $5.0B. The 10-Q does not name the customer. SEC blocks automated browsers without a declared contact, so slide 6 is a re-typed excerpt of the table (labelled "excerpt"), not a screenshot.
- Bloomberg (29 Oct 2026, citing a person familiar, via Seeking Alpha) and Semafor (30 Oct 2026) report that Customer B is Anthropic. Not confirmed by either company.
- Apollo release, 26 Oct 2026: SpaceX reached a binding commitment for an approximately $40B master finance lease for GPUs and related equipment with an Apollo-managed entity, for NVIDIA Vera Rubin chips. TechCrunch (26 Oct): leases start as equipment is delivered, from H1 2027; close expected by year end; CFO Bret Johnsen: "This lease diversifies our funding sources." Q3 capex nearly $20B, Q4 expected $35B to $40B.
- Bridgewater, note to clients quoted by Semafor (30 Oct 2026): "Concentration and acceleration are also risky in their own right."

Corrections to the source post:
- The $3.1B a month applies once all capacity is delivered (expected Dec 2026); fees differ during the ramp. "Up to $120.5B" is a ceiling.
- The agreements can be cancelled by either party on 90 days' notice after the initial periods end in Jan and Mar 2027 (the post leaves this out).
- SpaceX's filing does not name Anthropic; the identification is Bloomberg's report.
- The Apollo lease is for SpaceX's own buildout; neither the release nor TechCrunch ties it to the Anthropic contract.

## facebook

Anthropic's IPO filing contains one of the biggest compute deals disclosed so far: up to $120.5 billion with SpaceXAI, SpaceX's AI unit, over terms running into 2029.

Once all the capacity is delivered, which Anthropic expects in December 2026, that works out to about $3.1 billion a month (Anthropic S-1). At that rate, six months of SpaceX compute would cost about $18.6 billion, more than the $16.3 billion of revenue Anthropic booked in the first half of 2026. Its revenue is growing fast, though: the FT reported $16.8 billion for the third quarter alone.

There is an exit door. After the first non-cancelable periods end in January and March 2027, either side can cancel on 90 days' notice. By contrast, Anthropic's deals with Google, Amazon and Microsoft are minimums it owes regardless of usage.

On SpaceX's side, its 10-Q shows one customer made up 34.5% of third-quarter revenue of $14.58 billion. Bloomberg reports that customer is Anthropic. SpaceX has also agreed a roughly $40 billion lease for Nvidia Vera Rubin chips with an Apollo-managed entity.

Bridgewater, writing about how concentrated markets have become: "Concentration and acceleration are also risky in their own right."

Is this a smart bet for both companies, or too much riding on one customer?
