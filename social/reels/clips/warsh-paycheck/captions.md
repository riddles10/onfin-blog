16 September 2026. The Fed had just raised rates by a quarter point to 3.75 to 4 percent (Federal Reserve). A reporter asked what a hike does to people already squeezed by mortgage rates, gas and groceries.

Kevin Warsh said the least well-off tend to be people who don't own financial assets, which he put at "a bit less than 50%" of the country. His argument: stable prices let wages turn into "real take-home pay increases."

Higher rates raise borrowing costs but can slow inflation. Which matters more to you?
