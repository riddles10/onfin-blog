## instagram

In the 2008 financial crisis, the market for commercial mortgage-backed securities shut, and General Growth Properties, the second-largest US mall owner, could not refinance its debt.

The stock fell from $63 a share to 34 cents, Bill Ackman says. His fund started buying in November 2008, because he thought the assets were worth "substantially more than the liabilities." Lex Fridman cites reports that it turned $60 million into over $3 billion.

Ackman calls it an investment, not a trade. Most stocks that fall 99% never come back, and buying one takes deep research into what a company owns and owes.
